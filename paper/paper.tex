\begin{abstract}
This experiment evaluates K-means cluster structure in the full standardized 13-feature Wine Recognition space. PCA is used only for two-dimensional visualization, and known wine labels are reserved for post-hoc Adjusted Rand Index evaluation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Method}
K-means is tested for $k=2$ through $6$ with 30 initializations and seed 42. The selected $k$ maximizes silhouette score in the original standardized feature space. A separate two-component PCA projection is used for visualization.

\section{Results}
The selected solution is $k=3$. Full-space silhouette is 0.2849. The same cluster assignments appear more separated in the two-dimensional PCA view, with silhouette 0.5583. PC1 and PC2 explain 36.20\% and 19.21\% of variance. Post-hoc Adjusted Rand Index against the known classes is 0.8975.

\section{Interpretation}
The difference between full-space and projected silhouette scores illustrates why a visualization space should not automatically become the modeling space. The high ARI is descriptive only; known labels did not influence fitting or model selection.

\section{Limitations}
K-means assumes Euclidean compact clusters. Future work should compare clustering stability, Gaussian mixtures, density-based methods, and alternative validation criteria.

\section{Calculation audit and reproduction scope}
This unsupervised study separates clustering from visualization. K-means is selected using silhouette scores in all 13 standardized wine features, while a two-component PCA projection provides an interpretable view. The selected three-cluster solution has full-space silhouette 0.2849 and post-hoc adjusted Rand index 0.8975 against known labels; the much larger projected silhouette is explicitly not used as evidence that the full-space clusters are equally well separated.

a is within-cluster mean distance; b is the closest alternative-cluster mean distance. The two-dimensional silhouette is not the selection score. ARI uses known labels only after clustering and does not validate unseen-sample performance.
